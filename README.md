## Background
Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in women worldwide and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality. Adults aged 18-49 years account for a substantial proportion of cases but generally fall outside the age range targeted by routine population breast screening programmes, which typically begin in later adulthood. This population-based cohort study examines trends in breast cancer among individuals aged 18-49 years in the United Kingdom. Using routinely collected primary care electronic health records from the Clinical Practice Research Datalink (CPRD) GOLD, we describe the burden of breast cancer by estimating incidence, prevalence, and survival probability between 2000 and 2024. Breast cancer diagnoses are identified using validated SNOMED CT codes, and survival is estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Findings are stratified by age group, region, and calendar year to characterise patterns in disease burden and survival outcomes over time.

## Objectives
To estimate incidence and prevalence of breast cancer among individuals aged 18-49 years in CPRD GOLD between 2000 and 2024, overall and stratified by age group, calendar year, and UK region.

To describe the baseline demographic characteristics, comorbidities, and relevant medication use among individuals aged 18-49 years with breast cancer, using information recorded prior to and at the time of breast cancer diagnosis, and to compare these characteristics with age- and sex-matched individuals without a breast cancer diagnosis.

To estimate overall survival following breast cancer diagnosis in individuals aged 18-49 years, stratified by age group and calendar period of diagnosis.
